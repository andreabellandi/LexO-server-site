Welcome to LexO-server Suite

A RESTful service infrastructure designed for the collaborative management of linguistic resources modeled according to the Linked Data paradigm.

Enables the creation, editing, and deletion of complex dictionaries and lexicons, modeled as interoperable Knowledge Graphs.

🔗 Linked Data

Implementation of international W3C defacto standards such as OntoLex-Lemon, Lexicog, SKOS, and LexInfo for data interoperability.

🛠 RESTful Management

Robust API services for dynamic manipulation of lexical resources modeled as RDF graphs.

👥 Authoring

Integration with specialized authentication and user-management servers for user authoring of lexical elements, along with granular control over permissions and access rights (RBAC) across the entire dataset.

Data Model

LexO-server is built on a modular framework. While OntoLex-Lemon and Lexicog constitute the structural Core Models of the system, they are designed to be extended by Complementary Modules.

Core Model

OntoLex-Lemon fully implemented

The foundational standard for publishing lexicons on the Semantic Web, enabling the seamless integration between lexical data and ontologies. [Module Details]

Reference Paper: McCrae, J. P., Bosque-Gil, J., Gracia, J., Buitelaar, P., Cimiano, P. (2017) The Ontolex-Lemon model: development and applications. In Proceedings of eLex 2017 conference (pp. 19-21).
Core Model

Lexicog fully implemented

Designed to reflect traditional dictionary structures and the formal organization of lexicographic entries. [Module Details]

Reference Paper: Bosque-Gil, J., Gracia, J., Montiel-Ponsoda, E. (2017) Towards a Module for Lexicography in OntoLex. In Proceedings of the 1st Workshop on the OntoLex Model (pp. 74–84).
Complementary module

Decomposition fully implemented

Modeling the internal structure of compound words and multi-word expressions. [Module Details]

Reference Paper: Benzoni, E., Pellegrini, M., Dedè, F., Passarotti, M. (2024) Representing Compounding with OntoLex. An Evaluation of Vocabularies for Word Formation Resources. In Proceedings of the LREC-COLING Conference (pp. 13958-13969).
Complementary module

Variation & Translation partially implemented

Managing semantic relations between variants and cross-lingual translations. [Module Details]

Reference Paper: Bosque-Gil, J., Gracia, J., Aguado-de-Cea, G., Montiel-Ponsoda, E. (2015) Applying the ontolex model to a multilingual terminological resource. In Proceedings of the European Semantic Web Conference (pp. 283-294).
Complementary module

Syntax & Semantics not implemented

The interface between lexical items and their syntactic behavior and semantic argument structure. [Module Details]

Reference: W3C Ontolex specification.
Complementary module

FrAC not implemented

Integrating frequencies, attestations, and data from external corpora. [Module Details]

Reference Paper: Chiarcos, C., Apostol, E. S., Kabashi, B., Truică, C. O. (2022) Modelling frequency, attestation, and corpus-based information with OntoLex-FrAC. In Proceedings of the 29th International Conference on Computational Linguistics (pp. 4018-4027).
Complementary module

LemonEty partially implemented

Representing the history and evolutionary genealogy of lexical units. [Module Details]

Reference Paper: Khan, A. F. (2018) Towards the Representation of Etymological Data on the Semantic Web. Information 9(12), 304.
Complementary module

LexFom fully implemented

Representing lexical functions in the Meaning–Text Theory framework and for formally modelling the semantic and combinatorial relations between lexical units. [Module Details]

Reference Paper: Fonseca, A., Sadat, F., Lareau, F. (2016) Lexfom: a lexical functions ontology model. In Proceedings of the 5th Workshop on Cognitive Aspects of the Lexicon (pp. 145-155).
Riferimenti ufficiali: W3C Community | Lexinfo.net

Applications of LexO-server in Research Projects


VocaBO
It is the first native digital vocabulary devoted to the language of Giovanni Boccaccio, starting from the Decameron and designed to make its lexical richness openly accessible and searchable. The project combines scientific lexicography and Digital Humanities methods, enabling users to explore Boccaccio’s words, meanings, semantic fields, and textual occurrences through a digital environment.
OWNW
OWNW – Old Words for a New World: Translating Christianity to Baltic Pagans investigates how Christian religious terminology was translated and adapted in the earliest Baltic catechisms. It combines historical linguistics, religious studies, computational lexicography, and knowledge representation to build a diachronic computational lexicon of Baltic religious terminology.
ItAnt
ItAnt (Lingue e culture dell’Italia antica: linguistica storica e modelli digitali) investigates the cultures of ancient Italy through their linguistic documentation, especially epigraphic evidence from the 7th to the 1st century BCE, combining historical linguistics, philology, epigraphy, and computational methods. The project also developed digital tools and resources, including the DigItAnt platform, to support the creation, management, and interlinking of lexical and epigraphic data according to FAIR and Linked Open Data principles.
DiTMAO
DiTMAO (Dictionnaire de Termes Médico-botaniques de l’Ancien Occitan) is devoted to the creation of a dictionary of Old Occitan medico-botanical terminology, a medieval Romance language with a significant role in medical-scientific culture. The project combines lexicographic, multilingual, and encyclopaedic dimensions, using LexO-server/LexO services to model terms, variants, translations, conceptual references, and links to medico-botanical knowledge.
